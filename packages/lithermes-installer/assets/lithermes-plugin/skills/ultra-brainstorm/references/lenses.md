# Idea lenses for ultra-brainstorm children

Each delegate_task child gets exactly one lens from this list and a seed number. Child `i` (counting
from 0) takes lens `(i mod 34) + 1`, so two children in the same batch never look through the same
lens, and a run of 100 children uses every lens at least twice. A lens is a question the child can
answer alone, with nothing but the topic brief. Keep the list at 30 lines or more; add a line rather
than reword one, so an old run's lens numbers stay readable.

1. Opposite goal: aim for the reverse outcome; which part of that answer is actually right?
2. One rule gone: pretend one hard limit does not exist; what survives when it comes back?
3. Borrowed mechanism: which field far away already solved this shape of problem?
4. Hardest user: design for the most impatient, the least experienced, or the most expert person.
5. Take away: what can be deleted so the remaining parts work better?
6. Odd pair: join two things that are never combined here.
7. Ten years back, ten years on: how was it done before the current tools, and how after them?
8. Swap the scarce thing: replace the expensive resource with a plentiful one.
9. Fix the worst idea: write the worst possible answer, then reverse its worst trait.
10. Better question: answer the question behind the question.
11. By tomorrow: what works by tomorrow for almost nothing?
12. Tenfold: what would make the result ten times better, not ten percent?
13. Do nothing: when is leaving it alone the best option, and what does that cost?
14. Runs by itself: what could happen with nobody deciding each time?
15. By hand first: what would a careful person do on paper before any tool exists?
16. Change a default: which default, once flipped, changes what everyone does?
17. Safe failure: how does this break, and what design makes the break harmless?
18. Instant trust: what makes a cautious person say yes on first sight?
19. Count it: what can be measured, and which idea follows from the number?
20. Close the loop: where can each result feed the next attempt?
21. Neighbours: who else touches this, and what would they build on top?
22. One person, one day: the smallest real version.
23. A thousand at once: what changes with a thousand users, files or items?
24. Other medium: text, voice, picture, sound, motion, a physical object.
25. Other place: terminal, phone, paper, a meeting room, a car.
26. Who gains: who is rewarded today, and what if someone else were?
27. Reorder: change the order of the steps; what gets easier?
28. All at once or a trickle: one big batch, or many small pieces over time?
29. Borrowed ritual: a checklist, a handover, a habit from another trade.
30. Teach a newcomer: what makes this explainable in one minute?
31. No experts: what if nobody involved knew the field?
32. The shared belief: which thing everyone here assumes is probably wrong?
33. The constraint as a feature: make the hardest limit the selling point.
34. Undo first: design so every step can be reversed, then see what becomes possible.
