## Scope of tonight's lecture

- **The network**
  (1) 2,900 km of mains, laid from 1931 to 2024
  (2) One pipe in five older than 60 years
  (3) 410,000 litres a minute at the evening peak
- **The question**
  (1) How much treated water never reaches a tap
  (2) Why most of it leaves no visible burst
  (3) What a district can change in one year
- **Three parts**
  (1) Measuring the loss
  (2) Finding hidden leaks
  (3) Results from one pilot district
- **Who runs it**
  (1) One public utility, 1,140 staff, 410,000 customers
  (2) Water is treated at two plants and pumped to 14 districts
- Source: Example city water utility asset register (example)

## Acoustic survey vs pressure management

:::: columns 1fr 1fr
::: col
- **Acoustic survey**
  (1) Method: night listening on valves
  (2) Finds: single leaks within about 2 m
  (3) Cost: about $1,200 per km
  (4) Limit: plastic pipes carry sound poorly
:::
::: col
- **Pressure management**
  (1) Method: lower pressure at low demand
  (2) Finds: nothing; shrinks every leak
  (3) Cost: about $45,000 per valve
  (4) Limit: tall buildings may need own pumps
:::
::::

- Source: Example utility pilot reports (example)

## Pilot district programme, 2025-2026

| When | Step | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Jan 2025 | District meter | 9,400 m³ a day |
| Mar 2025 | Acoustic survey, 84 km | 61 leaks found |
| Jun 2025 | Repairs done | −1,300 m³ a day |
| Oct 2025 | Night pressure valve | −900 m³ a day |
| Mar 2026 | New meters, 2,100 homes | billing +4% |

- Each step measured on the district meter before the next began
- Source: Example utility pilot log

## Pilot district results after 15 months

| Daily supply | Loss share | Bursts a year | Repair cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7,200 m³ | 12% | 18 | $0.61m |
| before 9,400 m³ | before 27% | before 41 | payback 2.3 years |

- Daily supply down 2,200 m³, or 23%
- Loss share less than half, 27% to 12%
- Lower night pressure also halved new bursts
- Source: Example utility pilot evaluation

- A quarter of treated water never reaches a tap
- Most loss is small hidden leaks, not bursts
- Listening finds leaks; lower pressure shrinks them all
- One district cut its loss share from 27% to 12% in 15 months
- Lower night pressure also halved new bursts in that district
